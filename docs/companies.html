    About this data

    What this is

    A searchable atlas of every active property in USDA Rural Development's multifamily portfolio: Section 515 Rural Rental Housing, plus Section 514 off-farm and on-farm Farm Labor Housing. Each property carries its location, unit mix, Section 521 rental assistance count, tax credit status, ownership, and the year USDA projects its loan will pay off and the property will leave the program.

    Where the data comes from

    Three sources, all public domain:

    • Property Characteristics. The active projects workbook on USDA's Section 514/515 data page, with the public data dictionary.
    • Program Exit Data. The loan maturity and payoff projections on USDA's multifamily housing data index, with its own data dictionary.
    • HUD income limits. The Section 8 income limits workbook for the current fiscal year, covering every county and HUD metro area.

    The USDA files are joined on the MFIS project key, which is the borrower number, project number, and check digit combined. The join is close to complete. The handful of properties without Program Exit Data show a blank exit year rather than a guess.

    How to read the exit year

    USDA's estimated property exit year is the year the agency expects the property to leave the program, keyed to loan maturity. It is not the same as the loan payoff year, which reflects prepayment status and the remaining principal balance, and the two often differ. A property can also become eligible to prepay well before either date, which the prepay eligible fields capture. All three are shown on the detail panel so the timing question can be read properly rather than reduced to one number.

    Nothing here is a prediction of what an owner will do. It is USDA's own projection of when the regulatory clock runs out.

    How the Section 8 flag is built

    USDA's file carries no Section 8 indicator, so the flag comes from the PBS8 Database and is matched on street address, ZIP, county, city, property name and contract size. A match is only accepted when at least one strong piece of evidence lines up, and the detail panel says which. A blank is usually correct rather than a gap: HUD's 515/8 contract universe includes properties whose USDA loan has already paid off, and earlier phases of properties whose later phase is the one still carrying a loan.

    A Yes means the property carries a project-based rental assistance contract. Most are conventional Section 8. A minority are Section 202 PRAC or Section 811 project rental assistance, and those read Yes*. The distinction matters in one place: mark-up-to-market and the MAHRA renewal options are Section 8 mechanisms, so a starred property has no comparability-study path and its rents move by budget-based adjustment instead. Anything screening for mark-up-to-market should exclude the starred rows. The detail panel names the contract family for every match.

    Known limits

    • USDA refreshes these files a few times a year, and the two carry different as-of dates. Both dates are printed at the foot of the table and map pages.
    • Only active properties appear. Anything that has already exited is gone from the source file.
    • County names come from the Census county crosswalk. If that file has not been added to the build, the county column falls back to the five-digit FIPS code.
    • Coordinates are USDA's. A small number of properties have no usable coordinates and appear in the table but not on the map.
    • Unit counts are authorized units, which can differ from what is physically standing.

    Reuse

    The source data is federal open data in the public domain. The full joined dataset is available as CSV and GeoJSON.
